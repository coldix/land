# Fire Management - Hunting vs Management

Discussion point: "Fire management was fire hunting, calling it management is a stretch, but lots of small fires prevent large ones"

### What the literature says
- Phrase "fire-stick farming" coined by archaeologist Rhys Jones 1969 to describe way Indigenous Australians used fire regularly to burn land. This made hunting easier by herding animals.
- Same sources: sophisticated low-intensity burn, canopy protected, used to prevent wildfires and manage hunting grounds.
- Fire was used for many reasons: hunting, clearing dense bush to make travel easier, stimulating food plants, clearing around sacred sites.
- Jones coined phrase to describe overall impact of Aboriginal use of fire on Australian landscape.

### Interpretation
Both are true. Fire was used for hunting (driving game), but also deliberately to:
- Create mosaic of burnt/unburnt patches
- Promote fresh green pick to attract kangaroos
- Promote yams, bracken, cereals
- Reduce fuel load - preventing large wildfires
- Clear travel routes

Not agriculture in European sense (no surplus grain, no private fields), but not random. Best described as landscape management using fire as tool, with hunting as primary immediate benefit.

The observation "lots of small fires prevent large ones" matches modern fire science and is exactly what cultural burning literature describes.
