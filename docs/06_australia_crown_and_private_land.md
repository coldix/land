# Australia Timeline - Civilisation Arrives 1770, Crown Land and Private Land

## Before 1770 - Aboriginal Country
- 65,000-50,000 BCE to present - Aboriginal peoples inhabit continent. Estimated over 750,000 Aboriginal people in 1788. Land held as Country - rights/responsibilities by kin groups, not sold as commodity.

## 1770-1788 Crown claims everything
- 22 Aug 1770 - Cook at Possession Island: "I now once more hoisted English colours, and in the name of his Majesty King George the third took possession of the whole Eastern Coast"
- 1770 - James Cook claimed Australia for Britain under doctrine of terra nullius, land belonging to no one
- 26 Jan 1788 - First Fleet, Sydney Cove. All land vested in Crown. No private land until Crown grants it. This is Crown land.

## 1792-1820s First private land created
- 1792 - First grants to ex-convicts at Parramatta 30 acres to grow food. First alienation of Crown land to private freehold, only by Governor deed.
- 1800-1826 - Grants limited to Nineteen Counties around Sydney. Rest ungranted Crown land.
- 1824 claim west to 129 deg east, 1829 claim whole continent WA.
- 1826 Limits of Location - only buy Crown land inside settled districts.

## 1830s-1850s Squatting
- 1830s+ - Squatters take vast runs beyond limits, hundreds of square miles Crown land, grazing licences only. Become Squattocracy.
- 1835 - John Batman tries to buy 600,000 acres from Kulin people at Port Phillip. Governor Bourke declares void - only Crown can buy/grant land. Crown pre-emption.
- 1847 Order in Council - squatters get 14-year pastoral leases over Crown land. Not private ownership, but lease from Crown. Pattern for inland Australia - still Crown land leased.

## 1858-1860s Invention of modern private title
- 1858 Torrens Title - South Australia Real Property Act 1858. First sale registered 25 Aug 1858 to pastoralist William Ransom Mortlock. All colonies introduced Torrens between 1858 and 1875.
- Invented by Sir Robert Richard Torrens, Registrar-General of Deeds. Principle: title is in government register, not pile of old deeds. Makes private land easy to transfer/sell.
- Torrens Title System governs majority of land in Australia today.

## 1861-1890s Free Selection
- 1861 NSW Robertson Land Acts - Crown Lands Alienation Act and Crown Lands Occupation Act. Intention encouraging closer settlement and fairer allocation by allowing free selection before survey. Allowed those with limited means to acquire land. Anyone could select 40-320 acres unsurveyed Crown land at £1/acre on 5 shillings deposit. Broke squatters' domination.
- 1860s-1890s - Other colonies: Victoria 1860, Queensland 1868, SA Strangways 1869, WA Homestead 1893. Selection acts sought to lessen monopoly of squatters.

Result by 1900: private freehold around towns/wheat belt, vast Crown pastoral leases interior.

## 1901 to today
- 1901 Federation - Land stays with States. States own all un-alienated land as Crown land.
- Today roughly: ~50% private freehold Torrens Title (sellable), ~30-40% Crown pastoral lease (lease from Crown, you own improvements not soil), ~10% Crown reserves/national parks/state forests, remainder Aboriginal freehold and native title.

- 1966 Wave Hill Walk-off, 1976 Aboriginal Land Rights Act NT - first law to grant Aboriginal freehold based on traditional ownership.
- 1992 Mabo - On 3 June 1992 High Court handed down Mabo v Queensland (No.2) - first recognition of indigenous property rights at common law. Mabo (No2) was 1992 High Court case which legally overturned terra nullius fiction and recognised continuing connection. Court declared indigenous people had ownership long before European settlement, striking down terra nullius.
- 1993 Native Title Act - recognises some Crown land still has pre-existing Indigenous rights. 1996 Wik says pastoral leases (Crown leases) and native title can co-exist.

Summary: 1770-1788 Crown claims all. 1792 onwards Crown creates private land by grant. 1858 makes private land easily transferable via Torrens register. 1861 lets ordinary people select Crown land to make it private. 1992-1993 High Court says Crown claim did not wipe out all prior rights, so third category - native title - recognised on top of Crown and private.
